# EMTUPO: 10 Reel scripts (Oct 2026)

Each script names its formula, follows Point, Reason, Explain, Point (call to action), and ends on a comment keyword.
Hook scores are from hookscore.py (out of 100). Anything in {{ }} must come from your own practice; nothing is invented.

## 1. Before you extubate (hook 75 STRONG)
**Formula:** Better Way → Best Way  ·  **Keyword:** EXTUBATE

**Hook (said + on screen):** "Three things I check before I extubate. Number three is the one juniors skip."
On screen: 3 CHECKS BEFORE EXTUBATION

- **Point:** A good spontaneous breathing trial is not the same as a safe extubation.
- **Reason:** The trial tests the lungs. It does not test the airway or the cough.
- **Explain:**
  - Check one: the breathing trial. 30 to 120 minutes, no distress.
  - Check two: the RSBI. Breaths per minute divided by tidal volume in litres. Under 105 is reassuring.
  - Rehook: "But here's the one that catches people."
  - Check three: the cuff leak. Deflate the cuff. No leak means a swollen larynx, and stridor after the tube comes out. If there's no leak, giving steroids a few hours before extubation can help.
  - Then ask the question nobody writes down: can they cough, and can they clear their own secretions?
- **Point / CTA:** "Lungs, airway, cough. Miss one and the tube goes back in. Comment EXTUBATE for my one-page checklist."

## 2. Guidelines are not gospel (hook 80 STRONG), OPINION
**Formula:** Common Belief → Contradict  ·  **Keyword:** REALITY

**Hook:** "Stop treating guidelines as gospel when your ICU has half the kit."
On screen: GUIDELINES vs REALITY

- **Point:** Most guidelines are written for ICUs with everything. Copying them word for word in a low-resource unit can hurt patients.
- **Reason:** A guideline assumes the CT, the lab, the bed and the nurse are there at 3 AM.
- **Explain:** {{One real gap in your ICU, e.g. "no CT after midnight" or "one nurse for four ventilated patients"}}. {{What you do instead, and why it is still evidence-based}}. Rehook: "That's not cutting corners. That's knowing which part of the guideline is the medicine and which part is the equipment."
- **Point / CTA:** "Learn the principle, then adapt it to the bed you actually have. What's the one guideline your hospital can't follow? Tell me in the comments."

## 3. Posterior MI (hook 78 STRONG)
**Formula:** Common Belief → Contradict  ·  **Keyword:** POSTERIOR

**Hook:** "Your ECG shows no STEMI. Your patient is having one."
On screen: NO STEMI? LOOK AGAIN

- **Point:** A posterior heart attack hides behind ST depression.
- **Reason:** The standard 12-lead doesn't look at the back of the heart. It sees the injury in reverse.
- **Explain:**
  - Horizontal ST depression in V1 to V3, with tall R waves and upright T waves.
  - Most people call it ischaemia and move on.
  - Rehook: "Flip it in your head and it's ST elevation."
  - Move V4 to V6 round to the back as V7, V8 and V9. ST elevation of 0.5 mm or more there confirms it. That's a STEMI equivalent, and it needs the cath lab or thrombolysis, not a repeat troponin.
- **Point / CTA:** "ST depression in V1 to V3? Turn the patient. Comment POSTERIOR for the ECG card."

## 4. Calcium in hyperkalaemia (hook 60 OK)
**Formula:** Common Belief → Contradict  ·  **Keyword:** POTASSIUM

**Hook:** "Calcium does not lower potassium. Not by a single point."
On screen: CALCIUM ≠ LOWER K

- **Point:** Calcium protects the heart. It doesn't touch the potassium.
- **Reason:** It stabilises the cardiac membrane within minutes, but the effect wears off in 30 to 60 minutes.
- **Explain:** The plan has three jobs:
  - Protect: calcium, repeated if the ECG is still bad.
  - Shift: 10 units of insulin with glucose, plus salbutamol nebs.
  - Remove: binders, diuretics if they make urine, dialysis if they don't.
  - Rehook: "Here's where patients get hurt: the hypoglycaemia an hour after the insulin." Check glucose hourly.
- **Point / CTA:** "Protect, shift, remove. If you only did step one, the potassium is still there. Comment POTASSIUM for the protocol."

## 5. ROX index on high-flow (hook 80 STRONG)
**Formula:** Won't Work → Unless  ·  **Keyword:** ROX

**Hook:** "Saturation of 94% on high-flow does not mean your patient is safe."
On screen: 94% ≠ SAFE

- **Point:** On high-flow oxygen, the saturation can look fine while the patient is failing.
- **Reason:** We push the oxygen up and hide the work of breathing.
- **Explain:**
  - Use the ROX index: SpO2 divided by FiO2, then divided by the respiratory rate.
  - Example: 94% on 60% oxygen with a rate of 38 gives 94 ÷ 0.6 ÷ 38 = 4.1.
  - Rehook: "4.88 is the line." At 2, 6 and 12 hours, a ROX of 4.88 or more is reassuring. Below 3.85 means high risk of failure.
  - Don't let a late intubation become a crash intubation.
- **Point / CTA:** "Numbers can lie. The breathing rate tells the truth. Comment ROX for the calculator card."

## 6. The minute after intubation (hook 52 OK)
**Formula:** Results First (published data)  ·  **Keyword:** INTUBE

**Hook:** "4 in 10 intubations hit a dangerous drop in blood pressure. Here's how you prevent it."
On screen: 4 IN 10 CRASH

- **Point:** The tube going in is not the finish line.
- **Reason:** In the INTUBE study of nearly 3,000 critically ill adults (JAMA 2021), 45% had a major adverse event. Most of it was cardiovascular collapse.
- **Explain:** Before the drugs go in:
  - Check the shock index (heart rate over systolic). Above 0.9 is a warning.
  - Resuscitate before you intubate.
  - Have the vasopressor drawn up and connected, not on the trolley.
  - Lower the induction dose.
  - Rehook: "Then watch the five minutes after, not just the tube."
  - Positive pressure plus sedation drops the preload. That's when they arrest.
- **Point / CTA:** "Resuscitate, then intubate. Comment INTUBE for the checklist."

## 7. Correcting low sodium (hook 51 OK)
**Formula:** Won't Work → Unless  ·  **Keyword:** SODIUM

**Hook:** "Fix low sodium too fast and your patient can lose the ability to swallow. Here's the limit."
On screen: SLOW DOWN THE SODIUM

- **Point:** Correcting chronic hyponatraemia too fast can cause osmotic demyelination.
- **Reason:** The brain has adapted to the low sodium. Pull it up quickly and the cells shrink.
- **Explain:**
  - The limit is 10 mmol/L in the first 24 hours, and 8 in high-risk patients (alcohol, malnutrition, low potassium, liver disease).
  - Severe symptoms like seizures still need hypertonic saline now, aiming for a 4 to 6 point rise.
  - Rehook: "The trap is the patient who suddenly starts passing litres of urine." That water diuresis overshoots the sodium.
  - Check sodium every 2 to 4 hours. If it overshoots, bring it back down.
- **Point / CTA:** "Treat the symptoms fast and the number slowly. Comment SODIUM for the correction chart."

## 8. DKA and potassium (hook 58 OK)
**Formula:** Won't Work → Unless  ·  **Keyword:** DKA

**Hook:** "Starting insulin in DKA can stop the heart. Check this number first."
On screen: CHECK K BEFORE INSULIN

- **Point:** Insulin drives potassium into the cells, and the blood level can crash.
- **Reason:** The total body potassium is already low, even when the first result looks normal.
- **Explain:**
  - If potassium is below 3.5, hold the insulin and replace potassium first. (That's the 2024 consensus; older ADA guidance used 3.3.)
  - Start fluids first anyway.
  - Rehook: "And when the potassium is normal, it still needs to go in the bag."
  - Recheck every 1 to 2 hours.
- **Point / CTA:** "In DKA, potassium before insulin. Comment DKA for the first-hour card."

## 9. FEAST: fluids in African children (hook 61 OK)
**Formula:** Common Belief → Contradict  ·  **Keyword:** FEAST

**Hook:** "3,141 African children. Fluid boluses for shock killed more of them."
On screen: FLUIDS KILLED MORE

- **Point:** Fluid boluses are not automatically safe in children with poor perfusion.
- **Reason:** The FEAST trial (NEJM 2011) ran in Kenya, Uganda and here in Tanzania.
- **Explain:**
  - Children with severe febrile illness and poor perfusion who got fluid boluses had higher 48-hour mortality (about 10.5%) than those who didn't (7.3%).
  - Rehook: "This is not 'never give fluid'."
  - It means give fluid like a drug. Reassess after every aliquot. Watch the liver edge and the work of breathing. Follow WHO guidance for children with true hypotensive shock.
- **Point / CTA:** "Fluid is a drug. Dose it, then check it. Comment FEAST for the summary."

## 10. EtCO2 during CPR (hook 79 STRONG)
**Formula:** Better Way → Best Way  ·  **Keyword:** CAPNO

**Hook:** "Stop checking pulses to know if your CPR is working. Watch this number instead."
On screen: WATCH THE CAPNOGRAPH

- **Point:** Waveform capnography tells you more during CPR than your fingers do.
- **Reason:** End-tidal CO2 reflects blood flow to the lungs, which comes from your compressions.
- **Explain:**
  - Below 10 mmHg means your compressions need fixing: depth, rate, or swap the person.
  - Rehook: "Then watch for the jump."
  - A sudden, sustained rise often means ROSC, before anyone feels a pulse.
  - After 20 minutes in an intubated patient, a persistently low value is one sign among several that the outcome will be poor. Never use it alone.
- **Point / CTA:** "Better: pulse checks. Best: the capnograph. Comment CAPNO for the card."
